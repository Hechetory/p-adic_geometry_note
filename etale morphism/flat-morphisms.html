Flat Morphisms

Here we study the properties of flat morphisms that we will need as background for étale morphisms.

Flatness

Definition 1 (flat quasi-coherent sheaf over a base) Let \(\pi\colon X\to Y\) be a morphism of schemes and let \(\mathcal F\) be a quasi-coherent \(\mathcal O_X\)-module. We say that \(\mathcal F\) is flat over \(Y\) at \(x\in X\) if the stalk \(\mathcal F_x\) is flat as an \(\mathcal O_{Y,\pi(x)}\)-module. It is flat over \(Y\) if this holds at every point of \(X\).

On affine opens, this reduces to the familiar algebraic notion: if \(X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\), \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}(B)\), and \(\mathcal F=\widetilde M\), then flatness is controlled by the \(B\)-module \(M\) coming from \(B\to A\).

Lemma 1 With the notation above, \(\mathcal F\) is flat over \(Y\) if and only if \(M\) is flat as a \(B\)-module.

Proof. It is an easy commutative algebra exercise.

Corollary 1 If \(\pi:X\to Y\) is an affine morphism, and \(\mathcal F\) is a quasicoherent sheaf on \(X\), then \(\mathcal F\) is flat over \(Y\) if and only if \(\pi_*\mathcal F\) is flat over \(Y\).

Warningnonexample

The statement may fail even when \(\pi\) is quasi-compact and quasi-separated.

Consider the composite \[ \pi\colon \mathbb P^1_k\hookrightarrow \mathbb P^1_{k[x]} \to \operatorname{Spec}k[x], \text{ and set } \mathcal F=\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1_k}(-1). \] Then \(\mathcal F\) is a line bundle and hence flat over \(\mathbb P^1_{k}\).

On the other hand, the global section of \(\pi_*\mathcal F\) is \(H^0(\mathbf P^1_k,\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^1_k}(-1))=0\), which implies that \(\pi_*\mathcal F\) is not flat.

Definition 2 (flat morphism) A morphism \(\pi\colon X\to Y\) is flat at \(x\in X\) if the local ring \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\) is flat over \(\mathcal O_{Y,\pi(x)}\). It is flat if it is flat at every point, i.e. \(\mathcal O_X\) is flat over \(Y\).

Equivalently, by Lemma 1, \(\pi\colon X\to Y\) is flat if there exist affine open coverings \(X=\bigcup_i X_i\) and \(Y=\bigcup_i Y_i\) such that \(\pi(X_i)\subseteq Y_i\) for each \(i\), and \(A_i\) is flat over \(B_i\) whenever \(X_i=\operatorname{Spec}A_i\) and \(Y_i=\operatorname{Spec}B_i\).

Proposition 1 (flat morphisms form a reasonable class) Flat morphisms satisfy the following properties one expects from a geometric class of morphisms:

  1. isomorphisms and open immersions are flat;
  2. the composite \(\phi\circ\pi\colon X\to Z\) is flat whenever both \(\pi\) and \(\phi\) are flat;
  3. flatness is preserved by base change: if \(\pi\colon X\to Y\) is flat and \(\psi\colon Y'\to Y\) is any morphism, then \(X\times_Y Y'\to Y'\) is flat;
  4. flatness is local on both the source and the target.

Proof. The assersions 1 and 4 follow immediately by the definition. Assersion 2,3 follow from the corresponding properties for flat quasicoherent sheaves, see Lemma 2 and Lemma 3.

Lemma 2 Let \(\pi\colon X\to Y\) be a morphism, and let \(\mathcal F\) be a quasicoherent sheaf on \(X\) that is flat over \(Y\). Let \(\phi\colon Y\to Z\) be a flat morphism. Then \(\mathcal F\) is flat over \(Z\).

Proof. By definition, it suffices to check on stalks.

Lemma 3 Let \(X\to Y\) and \(Y'\to Y\) be morphisms. Let \(\mu\colon X'=X\times_Y Y'\to X\) be the projection, and \(\mathcal F\) a quasicoherent sheaf on \(X\) flat over \(Y\). Then \(\mu^*\mathcal F\) is flat over \(Y'\).

Proof. By definition, it suffices to check on stalks. Let \(x'\in X'\). Assume that \(x'\) maps to \(x\in X\), \(y'\in Y'\) and \(y\in Y\) respectively. Then \(\mathcal O_{X',x'}\cong (\mathcal O_{X,x}\otimes_{\mathcal O_{Y,y}} \mathcal O_{Y',y'})_{\mathfrak p}\) for some prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\). Then \[ (\mu^*\mathcal F)_{x'}\cong (\mathcal O_{X,x}\otimes_{\mathcal O_{Y,y}} \mathcal O_{Y',y'})_{\mathfrak p}\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\mathcal F_x. \] The rest is a commutative algebra exercise.

::: {prp-flat-exact name=““} Let \(\pi:X\to Y\) be a flat morphism, then the pullback \[ \pi^*:\operatorname{Qcoh}_Y\to\operatorname{Qcoh}_X \] is an exact functor. :::

Criteria of flatness

Proposition 2 (Tor criterion) A \(B\)-module \(M\) is flat if and only if

\[ \operatorname{Tor}_1^B(M,N)=0 \]

for every \(B\)-module \(N\).

Proposition 3 (ideal criterion) A \(B\)-module \(M\) is flat if and only if, for every finitely generated ideal \(I\subseteq B\), the natural map

\[ I\otimes_B M\longrightarrow M \]

is injective. The map sends \(a\otimes m\) to \(am\). Equivalently, we can only check \(N\) for finitely generated ideals of \(B\) in Proposition 2.

Proposition 4 Let \((B,\mathfrak n)\) be a local ring and \(M\) a finitely generated \(B\)-module. If \(\operatorname{Tor}(M,B/\mathfrak n)=0\), then \(M\) is free.

Proposition 5 (local criterion) Let \((B,\mathfrak n)\to (A,\mathfrak m)\) be a local morphism of Noetherian local rings, and \(M\) a finitely generated \(A\) module. Then \(M\) is \(B\)-flat if and only if \(\operatorname {Tor}^B_1(M,B/\mathfrak n)=0\).

Proposition 6 (slicing criterion for flatness on the target) Let \((B,\mathfrak n)\to (A,\mathfrak m)\) be a local morphism of Noetherian local rings, \(M\) a finitely generated \(A\)-module, and \(t\in \mathfrak n\) a non-zerodivisor on \(B\). Then \(M\) is flat over \(B\) if and only if

  1. \(t\) is not a zerodivisor on \(M\), and
  2. \(M/tM\) is flat over \(B/(t)\).

Proposition 7 Suppose \((B,\mathfrak n)\to(A,\mathfrak m)\) is a local morphism of Noetherian local rings, \(M\) is a finitely generated \(A\)-module, and \(t\in\mathfrak m\subset A\) is a non-zerodivisor on \(M/\mathfrak nM\). If \(M\) is flat over \(B\), then \(M/tM\) is flat over \(B\)

Closed immersion is well-behaved under the condition of flatness

Proposition 8 (Pullback of quasicoherent sheaves) Let \(0 \to \mathscr{F}' \to \mathscr{F} \to \mathscr{F}'' \to 0\) be a short exact sequence of quasicoherent sheaves on a scheme \(Y\), and \(\mathscr{F}''\) is flat. Let \(\pi\colon X \to Y\) be any morphism of schemes. Then the pulled back sequence \(0 \to \pi^*\mathscr{F}' \to \pi^*\mathscr{F} \to \pi^*\mathscr{F}'' \to 0\) remains exact.

Proposition 10 Let \(f\colon X\to Z\) and \(\beta\colon Y\to Z\) be morphisms of schemes, and let \(i\colon V\hookrightarrow X\) be a closed immersion. Set \[ W=X\times_ZY,\qquad V_Y=V\times_ZY, \] and denote the induced morphisms by \(\alpha\colon W\to X\) and \(i'\colon V_Y\hookrightarrow W\). If either \(\beta\) or the composite \(V\to X\to Z\) is flat, then there is a canonical isomorphism \[ \alpha^*\mathcal I_{V/X}\cong\mathcal I_{V_Y/W}. \] Equivalently, the pullback of the ideal sequence of \(V\) remains exact: \[ 0\longrightarrow\alpha^*\mathcal I_{V/X} \longrightarrow\mathcal O_W \longrightarrow i'_*\mathcal O_{V_Y} \longrightarrow0. \]

Proof. Since \(i\) is affine, affine base change gives a canonical isomorphism \[ \alpha^*i_*\mathcal O_V\cong i'_*\mathcal O_{V_Y}. \] The ideal sequence of the closed immersion \(i\) is \[ 0\longrightarrow\mathcal I_{V/X} \longrightarrow\mathcal O_X \longrightarrow i_*\mathcal O_V \longrightarrow0. \] If \(\beta\) is flat, then its base change \(\alpha\) is flat. Hence \(\alpha^*\) is exact, so pulling back the ideal sequence gives \[ 0\longrightarrow\alpha^*\mathcal I_{V/X} \longrightarrow\alpha^*\mathcal O_X \longrightarrow\alpha^*i_*\mathcal O_V \longrightarrow0. \] Using \(\alpha^*\mathcal O_X\cong\mathcal O_W\) and the affine base-change isomorphism above, this becomes the asserted exact sequence. Now suppose instead that \(V\to Z\) is flat. Then \(i_*\mathcal O_V\) is flat over \(Z\). By Proposition 8, we still have the exact sequence we desire.

Flatness and Smoothness

We will show that smooth morphisms are flat.

Proposition 9 Every smooth morphism of schemes is flat.

Proof. We first treat the case in which the schemes are locally Noetherian. Let \(\pi\colon X\to Y\) be smooth, let \(p\in X\), and put \(q=\pi(p)\). After replacing \(X\) and \(Y\) by suitable affine neighborhoods of \(p\) and \(q\), respectively, we may write \(\pi\) in the form

\[ \operatorname{Spec}\!\left( B[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+r}]/(f_1,\ldots,f_r) \right) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}B, \]

where the Jacobian matrix of \(f_1,\ldots,f_r\) has rank \(r\) at \(p\). Set

\[ S=B[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+r}], \qquad A_k=S/(f_1,\ldots,f_k) \quad (0\leq k\leq r), \]

and let \(p_k\in\operatorname{Spec}A_k\) be the image of \(p\). We prove inductively that \((A_k)_{p_k}\) is flat over \(B_q\).

For \(k=0\), the ring \((A_0)_{p_0}=S_{p_0}\) is flat over \(B_q\), since a polynomial algebra is flat over its coefficient ring and localization preserves flatness.

Suppose that \((A_{k-1})_{p_{k-1}}\) is flat over \(B_q\). Let \(t\) denote the image of \(f_k\) in \((A_{k-1})_{p_{k-1}}\). Since \(p\) lies on \(V(f_1,\ldots,f_r)\), we have \(t\in p_{k-1}(A_{k-1})_{p_{k-1}}\). We claim that \(t\) is a non-zerodivisor on the fiber

\[ C_k = (A_{k-1})_{p_{k-1}}/q(A_{k-1})_{p_{k-1}} \cong \left( \kappa(q)[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+r}]/(f_1,\ldots,f_{k-1}) \right)_{\bar p}. \]

The first \(k\) rows of the Jacobian matrix have rank \(k\) at \(p\). Consequently, the morphism

\[ \operatorname{Spec}A_{k-1}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}B \]

is smooth at \(p_{k-1}\), so \(C_k\) is reduced.

Choose variables \(x_{j_1},\ldots,x_{j_k}\) such that

\[ \Delta_k = \det\!\left( \frac{\partial f_a}{\partial x_{j_b}} \right)_{1\leq a,b\leq k} \]

is a unit at \(\bar p\). On the localized polynomial ring over \(\kappa(q)\), define a derivation by

\[ D(h) = \det \begin{pmatrix} \dfrac{\partial f_1}{\partial x_{j_1}} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial f_1}{\partial x_{j_k}}\\ \vdots & & \vdots\\ \dfrac{\partial f_{k-1}}{\partial x_{j_1}} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial f_{k-1}}{\partial x_{j_k}}\\ \dfrac{\partial h}{\partial x_{j_1}} & \cdots & \dfrac{\partial h}{\partial x_{j_k}} \end{pmatrix}. \]

For \(a<k\), two rows in the determinant defining \(D(f_a)\) coincide, so \(D(f_a)=0\). Thus \(D\) descends to a derivation of \(C_k\), and \(D(t)=\Delta_k\) is a unit in \(C_k\).

If \(g t=0\) in \(C_k\), then

\[ 0=D(gt)=D(g)t+gD(t). \]

Multiplying by \(g\) and using \(gt=0\) gives \(g^2D(t)=0\). Since \(D(t)\) is a unit, \(g^2=0\); since \(C_k\) is reduced, \(g=0\). Hence \(t\) is a non-zerodivisor on \(C_k\).

We may therefore apply the slicing criterion for flatness on the source to the local morphism

\[ (B_q,qB_q) \longrightarrow \bigl((A_{k-1})_{p_{k-1}},p_{k-1}(A_{k-1})_{p_{k-1}}\bigr), \]

with \(M=(A_{k-1})_{p_{k-1}}\). It follows that

\[ (A_k)_{p_k} \cong (A_{k-1})_{p_{k-1}}/t(A_{k-1})_{p_{k-1}} \]

is flat over \(B_q\). Induction gives that \((A_r)_{p_r}=\mathcal O_{X,p}\) is flat over \(B_q=\mathcal O_{Y,q}\). Thus \(\pi\) is flat at \(p\), proving the proposition in the locally Noetherian case.

We now remove the Noetherian hypothesis. Flatness is local on the source and target, so around any \(p\in X\) we may choose a standard smooth presentation

\[ U = \operatorname{Spec}\!\left( \bigl(B[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+r}]/(f_1,\ldots,f_r)\bigr)_{\Delta} \right) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}B, \]

where, after reordering the variables,

\[ \Delta = \det\!\left( \frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j} \right)_{1\leq i,j\leq r} \]

is invertible on \(U\).

Only finitely many coefficients of \(B\) occur in the polynomials \(f_1,\ldots,f_r\). Introduce an indeterminate \(c^i_{a_1,\ldots,a_{n+r}}\) for each such coefficient and let

\[ P = \mathbb Z \left[ c^i_{a_1,\ldots,a_{n+r}} \right]. \]

Define universal polynomials

\[ g_i = \sum_{a_1,\ldots,a_{n+r}} c^i_{a_1,\ldots,a_{n+r}} x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_{n+r}^{a_{n+r}} \in P[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+r}], \]

where each sum is finite, and put

\[ \Delta_0 = \det\!\left( \frac{\partial g_i}{\partial x_j} \right)_{1\leq i,j\leq r}. \]

The morphism

\[ U_0 = \operatorname{Spec}\!\left( \bigl(P[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+r}]/(g_1,\ldots,g_r)\bigr)_{\Delta_0} \right) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}P \]

is smooth. Since \(P\) is Noetherian, the first part of the proof shows that \(U_0\to\operatorname{Spec}P\) is flat.

There is a ring homomorphism \(P\to B\) sending each universal coefficient \(c^i_{a_1,\ldots,a_{n+r}}\) to the corresponding coefficient of \(f_i\). Under this homomorphism, \(g_i\) maps to \(f_i\) and \(\Delta_0\) maps to \(\Delta\). Hence

\[ U \cong U_0\times_{\operatorname{Spec}P}\operatorname{Spec}B. \]

Thus \(\pi|_U\) is obtained from the flat morphism \(U_0\to\operatorname{Spec}P\) by base change. Since flatness is preserved under base change, \(\pi|_U\) is flat. Such neighborhoods \(U\) cover \(X\), so \(\pi\) is flat.
