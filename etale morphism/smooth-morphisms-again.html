Smooth Morphisms Again

A deeper study of local models, fiberwise criteria, formal smoothness, and relative differentials.

Notation and local models

No global Noetherian hypothesis is needed below; the relevant finiteness assumptions are stated explicitly. For a morphism \(\pi:X\to Y\), write

\[ \Omega_{X/Y}=\Omega^1_{X/Y} \]

for the sheaf of relative Kähler differentials. If \(y\in Y\), then

\[ X_y := X\times_Y \operatorname{Spec}\kappa(y) \]

denotes the fiber over \(y\). A geometric fiber is the further base change \(X_{\bar y}=X_y\times_{\kappa(y)}\overline{\kappa(y)}\).

Definition 1 Let \(R\) be a ring and let

\[ B= \frac{R[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+r}]}{(f_1,\ldots,f_r)}. \]

The morphism

\[ \operatorname{Spec}B\longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}R \]

is standard smooth of relative dimension \(n\) if some \(r\times r\) minor of the Jacobian matrix

\[ J(f)= \left(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}\right)_ {\substack{1\le i\le r\\1\le j\le n+r}} \]

is invertible in \(B\). After reordering the variables, one may assume that

\[ \det\left(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}\right)_{1\le i,j\le r} \in B^\times. \]

A morphism is smooth of relative dimension \(n\) precisely when it is locally, on both source and target, of this form.

For a standard smooth presentation, the conormal sequence is

\[ (f_1,\ldots,f_r)/(f_1,\ldots,f_r)^2 \longrightarrow \bigoplus_{j=1}^{n+r}B\,dx_j \longrightarrow \Omega_{B/R} \longrightarrow 0. \]

The invertible Jacobian minor allows \(dx_1,\ldots,dx_r\) to be eliminated. Consequently,

\[ \Omega_{B/R}\cong \bigoplus_{j=r+1}^{n+r}B\,dx_j, \]

so \(\Omega_{B/R}\) is locally free of rank \(n\).

A zero-dimensional smooth immersion

Lemma 1 (Smooth locally closed immersions) Let \(f:X\to Y\) be a locally closed immersion. Then \(f\) is smooth of relative dimension \(0\) if and only if \(f\) is an open immersion.

Proof. Every open immersion is smooth of relative dimension \(0\), so only the converse needs proof.

There is a short conceptual argument. A locally closed immersion is a monomorphism. A smooth morphism of relative dimension \(0\) is étale, and an étale monomorphism is an open immersion.

The following affine calculation records the Jacobian argument behind this fact. Because the assertion is local on \(X\) and \(Y\), shrink around a point of \(X\) so that \(f\) is both a closed immersion and standard smooth of relative dimension \(0\). We may write

\[ X=\operatorname{Spec}B, \qquad Y=\operatorname{Spec}R, \qquad B=\frac{R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]}{(f_1,\ldots,f_n)}, \]

with

\[ \det J(f)= \det\left(\frac{\partial f_i}{\partial x_j}\right)_{1\le i,j\le n} \in B^\times. \]

Since \(X\to Y\) is a closed immersion, the ring map \(R\to B\) is surjective. Hence, for every \(i\), there exists \(r_i\in R\) and \(a_{ij}\in R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\) such that

\[ x_i=r_i+\sum_{j=1}^n a_{ij}f_j. \]

Differentiate with respect to \(x_\ell\):

\[ \delta_{i\ell} = \sum_{j=1}^n \left( \frac{\partial a_{ij}}{\partial x_\ell}f_j +a_{ij}\frac{\partial f_j}{\partial x_\ell} \right). \]

Modulo \((f_1,\ldots,f_n)\), this becomes the matrix identity

\[ I_n= (a_{ij})_{i,j} \left(\frac{\partial f_j}{\partial x_\ell}\right)_{j,\ell} \qquad\text{in }M_n(B). \]

The Jacobian matrix is invertible in \(B\), so \((a_{ij})\) is invertible in \(B\) as well. After possibly shrinking once more, the two ideals agree:

\[ (f_1,\ldots,f_n) = (x_1-r_1,\ldots,x_n-r_n). \]

Therefore

\[ B \cong \frac{R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]} {(x_1-r_1,\ldots,x_n-r_n)} \cong R. \]

Thus \(f\) is locally an isomorphism onto an open subscheme of \(Y\). Hence \(f\) is an open immersion.

Remark 1 (Étale interpretation). The lemma is often summarized as

\[ \text{étale monomorphism} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{open immersion}. \]

The relative-dimension-zero hypothesis is exactly what turns a smooth morphism into an étale morphism.

Equivalent criteria for smoothness

Theorem 1 (Fiberwise and differential criteria) Let \(\pi:X\to Y\) be a morphism of schemes, and let \(n\ge 0\). The following conditions are equivalent.

  1. \(\pi\) is smooth of relative dimension \(n\).

  2. \(\pi\) is locally of finite presentation and flat, its fibers have pure dimension \(n\), and \(\Omega_{X/Y}\) is locally free of rank \(n\).

  3. \(\pi\) is locally of finite presentation and flat, and every fiber \(X_y\) is a smooth \(\kappa(y)\)-scheme of pure dimension \(n\).

  4. \(\pi\) is locally of finite presentation and flat, and every geometric fiber \(X_{\bar y}\) is a smooth \(\overline{\kappa(y)}\)-scheme of pure dimension \(n\).

Proof. We follow the chain

\[ (1)\Longrightarrow(2)\Longrightarrow(3) \Longleftrightarrow(4), \qquad (3)\Longrightarrow(1). \]

Step 1: \((1)\Rightarrow(2)\)

Smooth morphisms are locally of finite presentation and flat. Locally, \(\pi\) has a standard smooth presentation

\[ B= \frac{R[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+r}]}{(f_1,\ldots,f_r)}. \]

By the Jacobian calculation following Definition 1, \(\Omega_{B/R}\) is free of rank \(n\). Standard smooth morphisms have fibers of pure dimension \(n\), so condition (2) follows.

Step 2: \((2)\Rightarrow(3)\)

Fix \(y\in Y\). Formation of Kähler differentials commutes with base change:

\[ \Omega_{X_y/\kappa(y)} \cong \Omega_{X/Y}|_{X_y}. \]

Thus \(\Omega_{X_y/\kappa(y)}\) is locally free of rank \(n\). Since \(X_y\) is of finite type and pure dimension \(n\), the differential form of the Jacobian criterion shows that \(X_y\) is smooth over \(\kappa(y)\).

Step 3: \((3)\Longleftrightarrow(4)\)

Smoothness and pure relative dimension are preserved by arbitrary base change, so (3) implies (4). Conversely, smoothness descends along the faithfully flat field extension

\[ \kappa(y)\subseteq \overline{\kappa(y)}. \]

Dimension is unchanged under algebraic field extension. Therefore (4) implies (3).

Step 4: \((3)\Rightarrow(1)\)

Fix \(p\in X\), and put \(q=\pi(p)\). Because \(\pi\) is locally of finite presentation, after shrinking around \(p\) and \(q\) we can write

\[ U=\operatorname{Spec}C \longrightarrow V=\operatorname{Spec}B, \qquad C=\frac{B[x_1,\ldots,x_N]}{I}, \]

where \(I=(f_1,\ldots,f_s)\) is finitely generated. Passing to the fiber over \(q\) gives

\[ \overline U = U\times_V\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(q) = \operatorname{Spec} \frac{\kappa(q)[x_1,\ldots,x_N]}{\overline I}. \]

By (3), \(\overline U\) is smooth of dimension \(n\) at \(p\). The Jacobian criterion therefore supplies

\[ r=N-n \]

elements of \(\overline I\), which we may take to be \(\overline f_1,\ldots,\overline f_r\), such that an \(r\times r\) Jacobian minor is invertible near \(p\). Lift these elements to \(f_1,\ldots,f_r\in I\), and localize so that the same minor is invertible. Set

\[ R= \frac{B[x_1,\ldots,x_N]}{(f_1,\ldots,f_r)}, \qquad J=I/(f_1,\ldots,f_r)\subseteq R. \]

Then \(\operatorname{Spec}R\to\operatorname{Spec}B\) is standard smooth of relative dimension \(n\), while

\[ C\cong R/J. \]

On the fiber over \(q\), the two schemes agree in a neighborhood of \(p\), so

\[ (J\otimes_B\kappa(q))_p=0. \]

Here is where flatness is essential. From

\[ 0\longrightarrow J\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow C\longrightarrow 0 \]

and the flatness of \(C\) over \(B\), tensoring with \(\kappa(q)\) remains exact on the left. Hence the displayed vanishing is genuine, rather than being obscured by a \(\operatorname{Tor}_1^B(C,\kappa(q))\) term. Equivalently,

\[ J_p=\mathfrak m_qJ_p. \]

Since \(J_p\) is a finite module over the local ring \(R_p\), Nakayama’s lemma gives \(J_p=0\). After shrinking around \(p\), we have \(J=0\), and hence

\[ U\cong\operatorname{Spec}R \]

is standard smooth of relative dimension \(n\) over \(V\). As \(p\) was arbitrary, \(\pi\) is smooth of relative dimension \(n\).

Remark 2. In a fiber of finite type over a field, closed points are dense. Thus one may also run Step 4 first at closed points of the fibers. The standard smooth locus is open, and a nonempty closed subset of a finite-type fiber contains a closed point; consequently, covering all closed points covers the whole fiber.

Formal smoothness

Definition 2 A morphism \(\pi:X\to Y\) is formally smooth if the following lifting property holds. For every affine \(Y\)-scheme \(T\) and every closed subscheme \(T_0\hookrightarrow T\) defined by a quasi-coherent ideal sheaf \(\mathcal J\) with \(\mathcal J^2=0\), the restriction map

\[ \operatorname{Hom}_Y(T,X) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_Y(T_0,X) \]

is surjective.

Equivalently, every commutative diagram

\[ \begin{array}{ccc} T_0 & \longrightarrow & X\\ \big\downarrow & & \big\downarrow\!\pi\\ T & \longrightarrow & Y \end{array} \]

admits a dotted lift \(T\to X\).

On affine schemes, with \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}R\) and \(X=\operatorname{Spec}B\), formal smoothness says: for every \(R\)-algebra \(C\), every square-zero ideal \(J\subseteq C\), and every \(R\)-algebra map

\[ B\longrightarrow C/J, \]

there exists an \(R\)-algebra lift \(B\to C\).

Theorem 2 A morphism \(\pi:X\to Y\) is smooth if and only if it is locally of finite presentation and formally smooth.

Proof. We first verify the lifting property for a standard smooth affine morphism. Let

\[ A=R[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+r}], \qquad I=(f_1,\ldots,f_r), \qquad B=A/I, \]

where an \(r\times r\) Jacobian minor is invertible in \(B\). Let \(C\) be an \(R\)-algebra, let \(J\subseteq C\) satisfy \(J^2=0\), and suppose that we are given

\[ \varphi:B\longrightarrow C/J. \]

Because \(A\) is a polynomial algebra, choose arbitrary lifts in \(C\) of the images of the variables. They define an \(R\)-algebra map

\[ \psi:A\longrightarrow C \]

lifting \(A\to B\xrightarrow{\varphi}C/J\). Since \(\psi(I)\subseteq J\) and \(J^2=0\), the restriction of \(\psi\) induces a \(B\)-linear map

\[ \overline\psi:I/I^2\longrightarrow J. \]

For a standard smooth algebra, the conormal sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow I/I^2 \longrightarrow \Omega_{A/R}\otimes_A B \longrightarrow \Omega_{B/R} \longrightarrow 0 \]

is split exact. Hence \(\overline\psi\) extends to a \(B\)-linear map

\[ \Omega_{A/R}\otimes_A B\longrightarrow J. \]

By the universal property of Kähler differentials, this extension corresponds to an \(R\)-derivation

\[ \delta:A\longrightarrow J \]

whose restriction to \(I\) equals \(\psi|_I\). Therefore

\[ \psi-\delta:A\longrightarrow C \]

vanishes on \(I\), so it factors through \(B=A/I\). The resulting map \(B\to C\) is the desired lift of \(\varphi\). Thus every standard smooth morphism is formally smooth, and therefore every smooth morphism is formally smooth.

Conversely, suppose that \(B\) is a finitely presented, formally smooth \(R\)-algebra. Choose a finite presentation

\[ B=A/I, \qquad A=R[x_1,\ldots,x_N]. \]

Apply formal smoothness to the square-zero extension

\[ A/I^2\longrightarrow A/I=B, \qquad \ker(A/I^2\to A/I)=I/I^2. \]

The identity map of \(B\) lifts to an \(R\)-algebra section

\[ B\longrightarrow A/I^2. \]

This infinitesimal section yields a splitting of the conormal sequence

\[ 0\longrightarrow I/I^2 \longrightarrow \Omega_{A/R}\otimes_A B \longrightarrow \Omega_{B/R} \longrightarrow 0. \]

Because the middle term is finite free, \(\Omega_{B/R}\) is finite projective. More concretely, fix \(p\in\operatorname{Spec}B\). After localizing, choose \(f_1,\ldots,f_r\in I\) whose classes form a basis of \(I/I^2\). Nakayama’s lemma shows that these elements generate \(I\) near \(p\). Since the inclusion

\[ I/I^2\longrightarrow\Omega_{A/R}\otimes_A B \]

is a split injection, its Jacobian matrix has rank \(r\); after shrinking, some \(r\times r\) minor is invertible. Thus \(B\) has a standard smooth presentation near \(p\). It follows that \(B\) is smooth over \(R\), and the scheme-theoretic statement follows by working affine-locally.

Remark 3. Formal smoothness alone is not equivalent to smoothness. For example,

\[ R[x_1,x_2,x_3,\ldots] \]

is formally smooth over \(R\), but it is not finitely presented over \(R\), so its spectrum is not smooth over \(\operatorname{Spec}R\) in the usual scheme-theoretic sense. Thus the precise statement is

\[ \boxed{ \text{smooth} \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \text{locally of finite presentation + formally smooth} }. \]

Injection of the morphism between fibres of the sheaves of differntials

Logical map of the note

The main implications can be kept in view as follows:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{standard smooth} &\Longrightarrow \Omega_{X/Y}\text{ locally free of the expected rank},\\ \text{flat + smooth fibers} &\Longrightarrow \text{standard smooth locally},\\ \text{standard smooth} &\Longrightarrow \text{square-zero lifting},\\ \text{finite presentation + square-zero lifting} &\Longrightarrow \text{smooth}. \end{aligned} \]

In relative dimension \(0\), smoothness becomes étaleness; imposing the monomorphism condition supplied by an immersion then forces the morphism to be an open immersion, as in Lemma 1.
