Unramified Morphisms

To study unramified morphisms, we need to understand the sheaf of differentials first.

Definition of the sheaf of differentials

For schemes \(X\) over \(Y\), there is a quasicoherent sheaf, \(\Omega_{X/Y}\), on \(X\). We call it the sheaf of differentials.

Definition 1 (sheaf of differentials) The sheaf \(\Omega_{X/Y}\) is globally defined as the quotient \(\mathscr I/\mathscr I^2\), where the ideal sheaf \(\mathscr I\) is the sheaf of ideals defining the diagonal embedding \(X\to X\times_Y X\). One can check that \(\Omega_{X/Y}\) is indeed a sheaf on \(X\). We define a map \(d\colon \mathscr O_X\to \Omega_{X/Y}\) by \(d(f)=\operatorname{pr}_1^*f-\operatorname{pr}_2^*f\) for \(f\in \mathscr O_X\).

Proposition 1 Affine locally, if \(X=\operatorname{Spec}(A)\) and \(Y=\operatorname{Spec}(B)\), then \(\Omega_{X/Y}\) is the sheaf associated to the \(A\)-module \(\Omega_{A/B}\), which is generated by symbols \(da\) for \(a\in A\) subject to the relations:

  1. \(d(a+a')=da+da'\) for all \(a,a'\in A\),
  2. \(d(aa')=a\,da'+a'\,da\) for all \(a,a'\in A\), and
  3. \(db=0\) for all \(b\in B\).

Proof. By definition, \(\Omega_{X/Y}\) is the sheaf associated to the \(A\)-module \(I/I^2\), where \(I\) is the kernel of the morphism \(A\otimes_B A\to A\). It is generated by elements of the form \(a\otimes 1-1\otimes a\) for \(a\in A\). One can check that the map \(\Omega_{A/B}\xrightarrow I/ I^2\) given by \(da\mapsto a\otimes 1-1\otimes a\) is an isomorphism of \(A\)-modules.

Remark 1 (the universal property). The module \(\Omega_{A/B}\) is universal for \(B\)-derivations from \(A\) to an \(A\)-module \(M\). We skip the proof here, but one can find it in any standard textbook on algebraic geometry.

Basic properties of \(\Omega_{X/Y}\)

There are two standard exact sequences of sheaves of differentials. The first one is the transitivity sequence: for morphisms \(f:X\to Y\) and \(g:Y\to Z\), there is an exact sequence \[ f^*\Omega_{Y/Z}\longrightarrow \Omega_{X/Z}\longrightarrow \Omega_{X/Y}\longrightarrow 0. \]

The second one is the conormal sequence: for a closed immersion \(i:X\hookrightarrow Y\) with ideal sheaf \(\mathscr I\), there is an exact sequence \[ \mathscr I/\mathscr I^2\longrightarrow i^*\Omega_{Y/Z}\longrightarrow \Omega_{X/Z}\longrightarrow 0. \]

NoteConormal sheaf

We call \(\mathscr I/\mathscr I^2\) the conormal sheaf of \(X\) in \(Y\). It is a vector bundle on \(X\) if \(i:X\hookrightarrow Y\) is a regular immersion.

The sheaf of differentials \(\Omega_{X/Y}\) is compatible with base change: for a cartesian diagram \[ \begin{array}{ccc} X' & \xrightarrow{\mu} & X \\ \downarrow & {\square}\,& \downarrow \\ Y' & \longrightarrow & Y \end{array} \] we have a canonical isomorphism \[ \Omega_{X'/Y'}\cong \mu^*\Omega_{X/Y}. \]

All these properties can be done by using Proposition 1 and the corresponding properties of \(\Omega_{A/B}\).

TipHint

For the two exact sequences, one can check the right one is indeed the cokernel. For the base change property, one can use the universal property of \(\Omega_{A/B}\).

Here is a property for \(\Omega_{A/B}\). For a localization \(S^{-1}A\) of \(A\), we have a canonical isomorphism \[ \Omega_{S^{-1}A/B}\cong S^{-1}\Omega_{A/B}. \] One can check this using the universal properties of the module of differentials and localization, or using the explicit description of \(\Omega_{A/B}\) in Proposition 1.

There is a proposition characterizing the sheaf of differntials for the fibre product. ::: {#prp-fibre-prd name=““} Let \(\alpha:X\to Z\) and \(\beta:Y\to Z\) be morphisms of schemes, and let \(W=X\times_ZY\), with projections \(p:W\to X\) and \(q:W\to Y\). There is a canonical isomorphism \[ p^*\Omega_{X/Z}\oplus q^*\Omega_{Y/Z} \xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_{W/Z}, \] given by the sum of the natural maps on differentials. :::

Proof. Write \[ u:p^*\Omega_{X/Z}\longrightarrow\Omega_{W/Z}, \qquad v:q^*\Omega_{Y/Z}\longrightarrow\Omega_{W/Z} \] for the natural maps. By transitivity and base change for differentials, we have an exact sequence \[ p^*\Omega_{X/Z}\xrightarrow{u}\Omega_{W/Z} \xrightarrow{s}q^*\Omega_{Y/Z}\longrightarrow 0, \] where we identify \(\Omega_{W/X}\cong q^*\Omega_{Y/Z}\). Naturality gives \(s\circ v=\operatorname{id}\).

Similarly, the natural quotient map \[ \Omega_{W/Z}\longrightarrow\Omega_{W/Y} \cong p^*\Omega_{X/Z} \] is a left inverse to \(u\), so \(u\) is injective. Thus the sequence above is short exact and split by \(v\). Consequently, \[ u\oplus v: p^*\Omega_{X/Z}\oplus q^*\Omega_{Y/Z} \xrightarrow{\sim}\Omega_{W/Z} \] is the desired canonical isomorphism.

Easy calculations of some \(\Omega_{A/B}\)

A useful lemma

Lemma 1 Let \(B\) be a ring and \(A=B[x_1,x_2, \cdots]/(r_1, r_2, \cdots)\) be the ring over \(B\). Then \(\Omega_{A/B}\) is a free \(A\)-module generated by \(dx_1, dx_2, \cdots\) modulo the relations \(dr_1, dr_2, \cdots\).

Proof. For the convenience of the proof, we assume that \(A=B[x_1, x_2, \cdots]\). The general case can be reduced to this case by using the transitivity sequence. Let \(M=\oplus_i A\,e_i\) be the free \(A\)-module generated by \(e_1, e_2, \cdots\). We define a map \(\phi\colon M\to \Omega_{A/B}\) by sending \(e_i\) to \(dx_i\) in \(\Omega_{A/B}\). One can check that \(\phi\) is surjective. To show that \(\phi\) is injective, we construct an inverse map \(\psi\colon \Omega_{A/B}\to M\). For any \(a\in A\), we can write it as a polynomial in \(x_1, x_2, \cdots,x_n\). We define \(\psi(da)\) to be the formal differential of this polynomial. One can check that \(\psi\) is well-defined and is indeed the inverse of \(\phi\).

Differentials for field extensions

Lemma 2 Let \(L/K\) be a separable field extension. Then \(\Omega_{L/K}=0\).

Proof. For any \(\alpha\in L\), there exists a polynomial \(f\in K[x]\) such that \(f(\alpha)=0\) and \(f'(\alpha)\neq 0\). Then \(f'(\alpha)d\alpha=df(\alpha)=0\), which implies \(d\alpha=0\). Since \(\Omega_{L/K}\) is generated by \(d\alpha\) for \(\alpha\in L\), we have \(\Omega_{L/K}=0\).

Lemma 3 Let \(L/K\) be a purely inseparable field extension. Then \(\Omega_{L/K}\neq 0\).

Proof. Assume \(\text{char}(K)=p>0\). By the transitivity sequence and the structure of purely inseparable extensions, we may assume \(L=K[x]/(x^p-a)\) for some \(a\in K\). By Lemma 1, we have \(\Omega_{L/K}\cong L\,dx/(px^{p-1}\,dx)=L\,dx\neq 0\).

Lemma 4 If \(L/K\) is separably genrated by \(t_1,\cdots,t_n\in K\), then \(\Omega_{L/K}\) is a free \(L\)-module generated by \(dt_1,\cdots, dt_n\).

Proof. By Lemma 1 and localization, we have \[\Omega_{K(t_1,\cdots,t_n)/K}\cong K(t_1,\cdots,t_n)\,dt_1\oplus\cdots\oplus K(t_1,\cdots,t_n)\,dt_n.\]

By Lemma 2, we have \(\Omega_{L/K(t_1,\cdots,t_n)}=0\), which implies \(\Omega_{L/K}\cong L\,dt_1+\cdots+ L\,dt_n\) by the transitivity sequence. It remains to show that \(dt_1,\cdots, dt_n\) are linearly independent over \(L\). We will prove this by showing that there exist \(K\) derivations \(d_i\) such that \(d_i(t_j)=\delta_{ij}\). For any \(i\), we can extend the derivation \(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_i}\) on \(K(t_1,\cdots,t_n)\) to a derivation \(d_i\) on \(L\) by using the fact that \(L/K(t_1,\cdots,t_n)\) is separable. To be precise, if \(\alpha\in L\) is algebraic over \(K(t_1,\cdots,t_n)\), then there exists a polynomial \(f\in K(t_1,\cdots,t_n)[x]\) such that \(f(\alpha)=0\) and \(f'(\alpha)\neq 0\). We can define \(d_i(\alpha)\) to be the unique solution of the equation \(f'(\alpha)d_i(\alpha)+d_i(f)(\alpha)=0\). One can check that \(d_i\) is indeed a derivation on \(L\) and \(d_i(t_j)=\delta_{ij}\).

Differentials for normalization

Let \[ A=k[x,y]/(y^2-x^2(x+1)),\quad B=k[t],\quad \operatorname{char} k\neq 2, \] with \(x\mapsto t^2-1\) and \(y\mapsto t(t^2-1)\). The induced map on differentials is \[ dx\longmapsto 2t\,dt,\qquad dy\longmapsto (3t^2-1)\,dt. \] Consequently, \[ \Omega_{B/A} \cong \frac{k[t]}{(2t,3t^2-1)}\,dt=0, \] since \(\operatorname{char}k\neq 2\) and \[ 1=\frac{3}{2}t(2t)-(3t^2-1). \] Hence the normalization morphism is unramified everywhere, including at \(t=\pm1\), the two points lying over the node.

Let \[ A=k[x,y]/(y^2-x^3),\qquad B=k[t], \] with normalization map \(x\mapsto t^2\) and \(y\mapsto t^3\). The induced map on differentials is \[ dx\longmapsto 2t\,dt,\qquad dy\longmapsto 3t^2\,dt. \] Therefore \[ \Omega_{B/A} \cong \frac{k[t]}{(2t,3t^2)}\,dt \cong \begin{cases} (k[t]/(t))\,dt, & \operatorname{char}k\neq 2,\\ (k[t]/(t^2))\,dt, & \operatorname{char}k=2. \end{cases} \] Thus the normalization morphism is unramified away from \(t=0\), but is not unramified at the point lying over the cusp.

Relation to the cotangent space

Proposition 2 Let \(X\) be a scheme over \(k\) and \(x\in X\) be a closed point with residue field \(\kappa (x)\) separable over \(k\). Then the cotangent space of \(X\) at \(x\) is isomorphic to \(\Omega_{X/k}\otimes \kappa(x)\).

Proof. Apply the conormal sequence to the closed immersion \(\operatorname{Spec}(k(x))\hookrightarrow X\), we have a map \(\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2 \rightarrow \Omega_{X/k}\otimes \kappa(x)\), where \(\mathfrak m_x\) is the maximal ideal of \(\mathcal O_{X,x}\). We will show this surjection is actually a bijection. For convenience, let \(A=\mathcal O_{X,x}\) ,\(k'=\kappa(x)\) and \(\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_x\). Since the module of differentials is compatible with localization, it suffices to show that the map \(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2 \rightarrow \Omega_{A/k}\otimes k'\) is a bijection. By Yoneda lemma, it suffices to show that for any \(k'\)-module \(M\), the map \[ \operatorname{Hom}_{k'}(\Omega_{A/k}\otimes k', M)\to \operatorname{Hom}_{k'}(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2, M) \]

is a bijection. By the universal property of \(\Omega_{A/k}\), we have a bijection \(\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\Omega_{A/k}, M)\cong \operatorname{Der}_k(A,M)\). By Lemma 2, we have \(\operatorname{Der}_k(k',M)=0\), which means that \[\operatorname{Der}_k(A,M)=\operatorname{Der}_{k'}(A,M)\cong \operatorname{Der}_{k'}(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2,M).\]

One checks that this is just the map defined above, and the last isomorphism will be shown when we discuss formally étale.

TipVariety case

In case when \(X\) and \(Y\) are varieties and \(x\) is a \(k\)-point, by Lemma 1, we could deduce that \(\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2\) and \(\Omega_{X/k}\otimes k\) both come from the cokernel of the Jacobian matrix. So they are isomorphic.

Unramified morphisms

Definition 2 (unramified morphism) A morphism \(\pi\colon X\to Y\) between schemes is unramified if it is locally of finite type and \(\Omega_{X/Y}=0\).

By the transitivity sequence, unramified morphisms are stable under composition. By the base change property, unramified morphisms are stable under base change. One checks that unramified morphisms are also local on the target, so they are ``reasonable’’ in the sense of the textbook the rising sea.

::: {prp-locus name=““} Let \(\pi:X\to Y\) be a morphism locally of finite type, then the locus in \(X\) where \(\pi\) is unramified is open. ::: :::{.proof} The locus is the complement of \(\operatorname {supp}(\Omega_{X/Y})\), which is naturally open. :::

Examples

By the above calculation of \(\Omega_{A/B}\), we have the following corollaries.

Corollary 1 If \(L/K\) is a field extension of finite type, then \(\operatorname{Spec}(L) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}(K)\) is unramified if and only if \(L/K\) is a finite separable extension.

Proof. By Lemma 2, it suffices to show that if \(\operatorname{Spec}(L) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}(K)\) is unramified then \(L/K\) is a finite separable extension.

If \(L/K\) is inseparable, then \(\Omega_{L/K}\neq 0\) by the transitive exact sequence and Lemma 3. Thus, \(L/K\) is separable. If \(L/K\) is trancendental, then \(\Omega_{L/K}\neq 0\) by Lemma 4. Therefore, the extension \(L/K\) must be algebraic separable, which impies that \(L/K\) is finite since it is algebraic and of finite type.

Example 1 (coincidence in number theory) Let \(\phi:(A,\mathfrak m)\to (B,\mathfrak n)\) is a local morphism of finite type between local rings. Then \(\phi\) is unramifed if and only if \(B/\phi(\mathfrak m)B\) is a finite separable extension of \(A/\mathfrak m\).

Proof. It is a immediate consequence of Proposition 4.

Criterions of unramifiedness

Proposition 3 Let \(\pi:X\to Y\) and \(\rho:Y\to Z\) be morphisms locally of finite type, and let \(\tau=\rho\circ\pi\). If \(\tau\) is unramified, then \(\pi\) is unramified.

Proof. The transitivity sequence gives a surjection \[ \Omega_{X/Z}\longrightarrow \Omega_{X/Y}\longrightarrow 0. \] Since \(\tau:X\to Z\) is unramified, we have \(\Omega_{X/Z}=0\). Therefore \(\Omega_{X/Y}=0\). As \(\pi\) is locally of finite type, it follows that \(\pi\) is unramified.

Proposition 4 Let \(\pi:X\to Y\) be a morphism locally of finite type. Then the following are equivalent.

  1. \(\pi\) is unramifed,
  2. for each point \(y\in Y\), \(\pi^{-1}(y)\) is the disjoint union of schemes of the form \(\operatorname {Spec} K\) where \(K\) is a finite separable extension of \(\kappa(y)\), and
  3. for each geometric point \(\bar y\in Y\), \(\pi^{-1}(\bar y)\) is the disjoint union of copies of \(\bar y\).

Proof. Assersion 1 implies 2 and 3 by Corollary 1 and base change. Assersion 2 (resp. 3) implies that \(\Omega_{X/Y}|_y=0\) (resp. \(\Omega_{X/Y}|_{\bar y}=0\)). Since \(\Omega_{X/Y}\) is of finte type, assersion 1 follows from the fact that if a quasicoherent sheaf \(\mathcal F\) of finite type on \(Y\) and \(\mathcal F|_y=0\) for all \(y\in Y\), then \(\mathcal F=0\). Note that \(\mathcal F|_y=0\) implies that \(\mathcal F_y=0\) by Nakayama’s lemma.

Proposition 5 Let \(\pi:X\to Y\) be a morphism between finite type schemes over an algebraically closed field \(k\). Then \(\pi\) is unramified if and only if \(\pi\) is injective on tangent vectors at closed points.

Proof. For any closed point \(x\in X\), let \(y\) be its image in \(Y\), then \(y\) is also a closed point \(Y\). By the transitive sequence, we have \[ \pi^*\Omega_{Y/k}\to\Omega_{X/k}\to\Omega_{X/Y}\to 0. \]

Pullback to \(x=\operatorname{Spec}k\), we have \[ \Omega_{Y/k}|_y\to\Omega_{X/k}|_x\to\Omega_{X/Y}|_x\to 0. \]

By Proposition 2 and the assumption, we know that \(\Omega_{Y/k}|_y\to\Omega_{X/k}|_x\) is the morphism between the corresponding cotangent spaces and hence surjective. Therfore, \(\Omega_{X/Y}|_x=0\), which mimplies that \(\pi\) is unramified at \(x\). Since the closed points are dense in \(X\), \(\pi\) is unramifed by ?@prp-locus.

Proposition 6 Let \(\pi:X\to Y\) be a morphism locally of finite type. Then \(\pi\colon X\to Y\) is unramified when its diagonal morphism \(X\to X\times_Y X\) is an open immersion.

Proof. We first record a lemma. Let \(i:Z\hookrightarrow W\) be a closed immersion whose ideal sheaf \(\mathcal I\) is of finite type. If \(\mathcal I=\mathcal I^2\), then \(i\) is also an open immersion.

Indeed, it is enough to work on an affine open subset \(\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq W\). Let \(I\subseteq A\) be the corresponding finitely generated ideal. For every prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\) containing \(I\), we have \[ I_{\mathfrak p}=I_{\mathfrak p}^2\subseteq \mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}I_{\mathfrak p}. \] Since \(I_{\mathfrak p}\) is finitely generated, Nakayama’s lemma gives \(I_{\mathfrak p}=0\). Consequently, \[ V(I)\cap\operatorname{Supp}(I)=\varnothing. \] Conversely, if \(\mathfrak p\notin V(I)\), then \(I_{\mathfrak p}=A_{\mathfrak p}\neq0\), so \(\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Supp}(I)\). Hence \[ V(I)=\operatorname{Spec}A\setminus\operatorname{Supp}(I). \] Because \(I\) is finitely generated, \(\operatorname{Supp}(I)\) is closed. Thus \(V(I)\) is open, proving the lemma.

We now apply the lemma to the diagonal. Since \(\pi\) is locally of finite type, \(\Delta_\pi\) is locally of finite presentation. To see this locally, write \[ B\longrightarrow A=B[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/J. \] The multiplication map \(A\otimes_BA\longrightarrow A\) has kernel generated by the finitely many elements \[ x_i\otimes1-1\otimes x_i,\qquad 1\leq i\leq n. \] Thus the diagonal is, locally on its source and target, a closed immersion defined by an ideal sheaf \(\mathcal I\) of finite type.

Suppose first that \(\pi\) is unramified. Then \(\Omega_{X/Y}=0\). Since \(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2\cong\Omega_{X/Y}\), it follows that \(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2=0\), and hence \(\mathcal I=\mathcal I^2\). The lemma therefore shows, locally around every point of \(\Delta_f(X)\), that the diagonal is an open immersion. Hence \(\Delta_\pi\) is an open immersion.

Conversely, suppose that \(\Delta_\pi\) is an open immersion. An open immersion has zero conormal sheaf, so \(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2=0\), which implies that \(\Omega_{X/Y}=0\). Since \(\pi\) is locally of finite type, this is precisely the condition that \(\pi\) be unramified.

::: {cor-cancellation name=““} Let \(\mathcal P\) be a class of morphisms stable under base change and restriction to open subschemes of the source. Let \(\pi:X\to Y\) and \(\rho:Y\to Z\) be morphisms, and set \(\tau=\rho\circ\pi\). If \(\rho\) is unramified and \(\tau\) belongs to \(\mathcal P\), then \(\pi\) belongs to \(\mathcal P\).

In particular, if \(\tau\) is smooth of relative dimension \(n\) (resp. étale), then so is \(\pi\). :::

Proof. Factor \(\pi\) as \[ X\xrightarrow{\Gamma_\pi}X\times_ZY \xrightarrow{\operatorname{pr}_2}Y, \qquad \Gamma_\pi=(\operatorname{id}_X,\pi). \] The square \[ \begin{array}{ccc} X & \xrightarrow{\Gamma_\pi} & X\times_ZY\\ {\scriptstyle\pi}\downarrow && \downarrow{\scriptstyle(\pi\circ\operatorname{pr}_1,\operatorname{pr}_2)}\\ Y & \xrightarrow{\Delta_\rho} & Y\times_ZY \end{array} \] is Cartesian. Since \(\rho\) is unramified, \(\Delta_\rho\) is an open immersion, and hence so is \(\Gamma_\pi\).

Moreover, \(\operatorname{pr}_2\) is the base change of \(\tau\) along \(\rho\), so it belongs to \(\mathcal P\). Thus \(\pi=\operatorname{pr}_2\circ\Gamma_\pi\) belongs to \(\mathcal P\) by stability under restriction to open subschemes of the source.
