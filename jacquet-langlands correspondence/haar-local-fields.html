Haar Measure and Local Fields

From Radon measures to characteristic-zero local fields

Radon and Haar measures, characteristic-zero local fields, valuation theory, change of variables, and the upper-triangular group in \(\mathrm{GL}_2\).

1 Haar measure

Let \(X\) be a locally compact Hausdorff space, let \(\mathcal B(X)\) denote its Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra, and let

\[ C_c(X)=\{f:X\to\mathbb R : f \text{ is continuous and compactly supported}\}. \]

1.1 Radon measures and positive linear functionals

Definition 1.1 (Radon measure) A Radon measure on \(X\) is a positive Borel measure \(\mu\) such that

  1. \(\mu(K)<\infty\) for every compact set \(K\subseteq X\);
  2. \(\mu\) is inner regular on open sets: \[ \mu(U)=\sup\{\mu(K):K\subseteq U,\ K\text{ compact}\}; \]
  3. \(\mu\) is outer regular on Borel sets: \[ \mu(E)=\inf\{\mu(U):E\subseteq U,\ U\text{ open}\}. \]

On a locally compact Hausdorff space, these regularity conditions make integration against \(\mu\) compatible with the topology of \(X\).

Definition 1.2 (Positive linear functional on C_c(X)) A linear map

\[ \Lambda:C_c(X)\longrightarrow \mathbb R \]

is positive if

\[ f\ge 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Lambda(f)\ge 0. \]

Positivity already implies the local boundedness needed in the Riesz representation theorem: for every compact \(K\subseteq X\), the restriction of \(\Lambda\) to functions supported in \(K\) is bounded in the uniform norm.

Theorem 1.1 (Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem) The assignment

\[ \mu\longmapsto \Lambda_\mu, \qquad \Lambda_\mu(f)=\int_X f(x)\,d\mu(x), \]

is a bijection between Radon measures on \(X\) and positive linear functionals on \(C_c(X)\).

In particular, every positive linear functional \(\Lambda\) has the form

\[ \Lambda(f)=\int_X f\,d\mu \]

for a unique Radon measure \(\mu\).

The uniqueness is especially useful for invariant measures: if a positive functional is invariant under a group action on \(C_c(X)\), its representing Radon measure is invariant under the corresponding action on \(X\).

1.2 Haar measure on a locally compact group

From now on, let \(G\) be a locally compact Hausdorff topological group.

Definition 1.3 (Haar measure) A left Haar measure on \(G\) is a nonzero Radon measure \(\mu_L\) satisfying

\[ \mu_L(gE)=\mu_L(E) \]

for every \(g\in G\) and every Borel set \(E\subseteq G\).

A right Haar measure on \(G\) is a nonzero Radon measure \(\mu_R\) satisfying

\[ \mu_R(Eg)=\mu_R(E). \]

Remark 1.1 (The two translation actions on C_c(G)). There are two natural actions of \(G\) on \(C_c(G)\):

\[ (L_gf)(x)=f(g^{-1}x), \qquad (R_gf)(x)=f(xg). \]

A measure \(\mu_L\) is left Haar exactly when

\[ \int_G L_gf\,d\mu_L=\int_G f\,d\mu_L \]

for all \(g\in G\) and \(f\in C_c(G)\). The analogous statement with \(R_g\) characterizes right Haar measure.

Theorem 1.2 (Haar-Weil theorem: existence) Every locally compact Hausdorff group admits a left Haar measure and a right Haar measure.

The theorem is often proved by first constructing a nonzero positive translation-invariant functional on \(C_c(G)\) and then applying Theorem 1.1.

1.2.1 Passing between left and right Haar measures

For a function \(f\in C_c(G)\) and a Borel set \(E\subseteq G\), set

\[ \check f(g)=f(g^{-1}), \qquad E^{-1}=\{g^{-1}:g\in E\}. \]

If \(\mu\) is right Haar, define

\[ \check\mu(E)=\mu(E^{-1}). \]

Then \(\check\mu\) is left Haar. Conversely, inversion sends left Haar measure to right Haar measure. In integral form,

\[ \int_G f(g)\,d\check\mu(g) = \int_G \check f(g)\,d\mu(g) = \int_G f(g^{-1})\,d\mu(g). \]

1.3 Uniqueness and the automorphism modulus

Theorem 1.3 (Uniqueness of Haar measure) Any two left Haar measures on \(G\) differ by multiplication by a positive scalar. The same is true of right Haar measures.

Fix a right Haar measure \(\mu\) on \(G\), and let

\[ \operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{cts}}(G) \]

be the group of continuous automorphisms of \(G\).

Proposition 1.1 (Modulus of an automorphism) For each \(u\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{cts}}(G)\), there is a unique number \(\delta(u)>0\) such that

\[ \mu(u(E))=\delta(u)\mu(E) \]

for every Borel set \(E\subseteq G\).

The number \(\delta(u)\) is independent of the chosen right Haar measure, and

\[ \delta:\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{cts}}(G)\longrightarrow\mathbb R_{>0} \]

is a group homomorphism. Equipped with the Braconnier topology, \(\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{cts}}(G)\) is a topological group and \(\delta\) is continuous. Thus

\[ \delta(u_1u_2)=\delta(u_1)\delta(u_2). \]

Equivalently, for every \(f\in C_c(G)\),

\[ \int_G f(u(x))\,d\mu(x) = \delta(u)^{-1}\int_G f(x)\,d\mu(x). \]

Indeed, the measure \(E\mapsto\mu(u(E))\) is again right Haar, so Theorem 1.3 supplies the scalar. Replacing \(\mu\) by \(c\mu\) does not change that scalar, and composition of automorphisms proves multiplicativity.

Definition 1.4 (Modular character) The conjugation map is

\[ \operatorname{Ad}:G\longrightarrow\operatorname{Inn}(G) \subseteq\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathrm{cts}}(G), \qquad \operatorname{Ad}(g)(x)=gxg^{-1}. \]

The modular character of \(G\) is

\[ \delta_G:G\longrightarrow\mathbb R_{>0}, \qquad \delta_G(g)=\delta(\operatorname{Ad}(g)). \]

This convention is tied to the fixed right Haar measure: \(\mu(gE)=\delta_G(g)\mu(E)\). Authors who begin with a left Haar measure sometimes use the reciprocal function, so the defining change-of-variables identity should always be checked.

1.3.1 Useful formulas

Let \(E\in\mathcal B(G)\) and \(g\in G\). Since \(\mu\) is right invariant,

\[ \mu(gE) = \mu(gEg^{-1}) = \delta_G(g)\mu(E). \]

Likewise, for \(f\in C_c(G)\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_G f(gx)\,d\mu(x) &=\int_G f(gxg^{-1})\,d\mu(x)\\ &=\delta_G(g)^{-1}\int_G f(x)\,d\mu(x). \end{aligned} \]

The first equality uses right invariance, while the second is the change-of-variables formula for \(\operatorname{Ad}(g)\).

1.4 Unimodularity and examples

Definition 1.5 (Unimodular group) The group \(G\) is unimodular if

\[ \delta_G\equiv 1. \]

Equivalently, every right Haar measure is also left Haar.

Example 1.1 (Basic unimodular groups) Each of the following is unimodular:

  • every abelian locally compact group, because every inner automorphism is the identity;
  • every compact group, because the image of the continuous homomorphism \(\delta_G:G\to\mathbb R_{>0}\) is compact, and the only compact subgroup of \(\mathbb R_{>0}\) is \(\{1\}\);
  • every discrete group, because counting measure is both left and right invariant.

Example 1.2 (Lebesgue measure on R^n) The additive group \(\mathbb R^n\) is unimodular. Its automorphism group is \(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb R)\), and Lebesgue measure satisfies

\[ \delta_{\mathbb R^n}(A)=|\det A|, \qquad A\in\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb R). \]

Here \(\delta_{\mathbb R^n}(A)\) is the automorphism modulus from Proposition 1.1, not the modular character of the abelian group \(\mathbb R^n\).

Example 1.3 (Haar measure on R^times) The measure

\[ d^\times x=\frac{dx}{|x|} \]

is Haar measure on the multiplicative group \(\mathbb R^\times\). Indeed, the substitution \(x=ay\) gives

\[ \frac{dx}{|x|}=\frac{|a|\,dy}{|a||y|}=\frac{dy}{|y|}. \]

Example 1.4 (The upper-triangular subgroup) Let

\[ B= \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a&b\\ 0&c \end{pmatrix} :a,c\in\mathbb R^\times,\ b\in\mathbb R \right\} \subseteq\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb R). \]

Then

\[ \delta_B\!\left( \begin{pmatrix} a&b\\ 0&c \end{pmatrix} \right) = \left|\frac{a}{c}\right|. \]

The same formula holds over an arbitrary local field after replacing the ordinary absolute value by the normalized absolute value of that field. A direct calculation appears at the end of the note.

2 Local fields of characteristic zero

Let \(F\) be a locally compact Hausdorff topological field of characteristic zero. Its additive group \((F,+)\) is locally compact and abelian.

2.1 The normalized absolute value from Haar measure

For \(a\in F^\times\), multiplication by \(a\) is a continuous automorphism

\[ m_a:(F,+)\longrightarrow(F,+), \qquad x\longmapsto ax. \]

Definition 2.1 (Module of multiplication) Define

\[ \lVert a\rVert_F=\delta_{(F,+)}(m_a)>0 \qquad(a\in F^\times), \]

and set \(\lVert0\rVert_F=0\). We call \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_F\) the Haar-normalized absolute value, or module, of \(F\).

By construction,

\[ \lVert ab\rVert_F=\lVert a\rVert_F\lVert b\rVert_F. \]

If \(\mu_F\) is an additive Haar measure, the defining change-of-variables identities are

\[ \mu_F(aE)=\lVert a\rVert_F\mu_F(E) \]

and

\[ \int_F f(ax)\,d\mu_F(x) = \lVert a\rVert_F^{-1}\int_F f(x)\,d\mu_F(x). \]

Proposition 2.1 (Associated ordinary absolute value) With the standard local-field normalization, there is a number \(t_F>0\) such that

\[ |x|=\lVert x\rVert_F^{1/t_F} \]

is an ordinary absolute value inducing the original topology on \(F\). In the classification below,

\[ t_F= \begin{cases} 2,&F\cong\mathbb C,\\ 1,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

Thus the exceptional case is

\[ \lVert z\rVert_{\mathbb C}=z\overline z=|z|^2. \]

Remark 2.1 (A normalization point). Without fixing a convention, the exponent in Proposition 2.1 is not unique: positive powers can define equivalent absolute values. The values of \(t_F\) above are the standard choices compatible with Haar measure and the usual absolute value on \(\mathbb R\) and \(\mathbb C\); in the nonarchimedean case, the module \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_F\) itself is used.

The metric

\[ d(x,y)=|x-y| \]

induces the original topology of \(F\). We exclude the discrete topology; in the discrete case the corresponding module is trivial:

\[ \lVert x\rVert_F= \begin{cases} 1,&x\ne0,\\ 0,&x=0. \end{cases} \]

Definition 2.2 (Local field of characteristic zero) A local field of characteristic zero is a nondiscrete, locally compact Hausdorff topological field \(F\) with \(\operatorname{char}F=0\).

2.2 Classification of characteristic-zero local fields

The prime field gives an embedding

\[ \mathbb Q\hookrightarrow F. \]

The restriction of \(|\cdot|\) to \(\mathbb Q\) is nontrivial. Indeed, choose a compact neighborhood \(K\) of \(0\) and a radius \(r>0\) such that \(\overline B(0,r)\subseteq K\). Using nondiscreteness, choose \(x\ne0\) with \(|x|<r\). If \(|n|=1\) for every nonzero \(n\in\mathbb Z\), then all points \(nx\) lie in the compact set \(\overline B(0,r)\) and

\[ |nx-mx|=|n-m|\,|x|=|x| \]

whenever \(n\ne m\). This would give an infinite \(|x|\)-separated subset of a compact metric space, which is impossible.

Restricting the associated absolute value to \(\mathbb Q\) and applying Ostrowski’s theorem now shows that exactly one of the following occurs:

\[ |\cdot|\big|_{\mathbb Q} \sim |\cdot|_\infty \qquad\text{or}\qquad |\cdot|\big|_{\mathbb Q} \sim |\cdot|_p \]

for a unique prime \(p\). Here \(\sim\) means that one absolute value is a positive power of the other; equivalent absolute values determine the same topology.

Proposition 2.2 (Completeness) The field \(F\) is complete for the metric \(d(x,y)=|x-y|\).

Proof. Translations act transitively on \(F\) by isometries. Choose a compact neighborhood \(K\) of \(0\). Since the metric topology is the given topology, some closed ball \(\overline B(0,r)\) is contained in \(K\) and is therefore compact. Translation then shows that every closed ball

\[ \overline B(x,r)=x+\overline B(0,r) \]

is compact. If \((x_n)\) is Cauchy, then some tail lies in one such compact ball. That tail has a convergent subsequence, and a Cauchy sequence with a convergent subsequence converges to the same limit. Hence \(F\) is complete. \(\square\)

Consequently, the closure of \(\mathbb Q\) in \(F\) is isomorphic either to \(\mathbb R\) or to \(\mathbb Q_p\). Thus \(F\) is a topological vector space over one of these two complete fields.

Theorem 2.1 (Weil’s finite-dimensionality theorem) Let \(K\) be a nondiscrete locally compact field. If \(V\) is a Hausdorff topological \(K\)-vector space that is locally compact, then

\[ \dim_KV<\infty. \]

Applying Theorem 2.1 to \(V=F\) gives

\[ [F:\mathbb R]<\infty \qquad\text{or}\qquad [F:\mathbb Q_p]<\infty. \]

We arrive at the classification.

Theorem 2.2 (Classification) Every characteristic-zero local field is isomorphic, as a topological field, to exactly one of the following types:

  1. an archimedean local field, namely \(\mathbb R\) or \(\mathbb C\);
  2. a nonarchimedean local field, namely a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\) for a unique prime \(p\).

For \(F=\mathbb R\), one has \(t_F=1\). For \(F=\mathbb C\), one has \(t_F=2\) because multiplication by \(z\) on the real vector space \(\mathbb C\cong\mathbb R^2\) has determinant \(z\overline z\). For a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\), the module \(\lVert\cdot\rVert_F\) is already nonarchimedean, so the standard choice is \(t_F=1\).

3 Valuation theory in the nonarchimedean case

Assume from now on that \(F\) is a finite extension of \(\mathbb Q_p\). Write \(|\cdot|_F\) for its Haar-normalized nonarchimedean absolute value; thus \(|\cdot|_F=\lVert\cdot\rVert_F\).

3.1 The valuation ring, maximal ideal, and uniformizer

Define

\[ \mathcal O_F=\{x\in F:|x|_F\le1\}, \qquad \mathfrak p_F=\{x\in F:|x|_F<1\}. \]

Then \(\mathcal O_F\) is a compact open subring of \(F\), and

\[ \mathcal O_F^\times=\{x\in F:|x|_F=1\}. \]

The ring \(\mathcal O_F\) is a discrete valuation ring. Its unique maximal ideal is \(\mathfrak p_F\), and there is a uniformizer \(\varpi_F\in\mathfrak p_F\) such that

\[ \mathfrak p_F=\varpi_F\mathcal O_F. \]

Every \(x\in F^\times\) admits a unique decomposition

\[ x=\varpi_F^{v_F(x)}u, \qquad v_F(x)\in\mathbb Z, \quad u\in\mathcal O_F^\times. \]

Equivalently,

\[ F^\times=\varpi_F^{\mathbb Z}\times\mathcal O_F^\times, \]

and the normalized valuation

\[ v_F:F^\times\longrightarrow\mathbb Z \]

is characterized by \(v_F(\varpi_F)=1\).

The residue field

\[ k_F=\mathcal O_F/\mathfrak p_F \]

is finite. Put

\[ q=\#k_F. \]

Because \([\mathcal O_F:\mathfrak p_F]=q\), additive Haar measure normalized by \(\mu_F(\mathcal O_F)=1\) satisfies

\[ \mu_F(\mathfrak p_F)=q^{-1}. \]

It follows that

\[ |x|_F=q^{-v_F(x)}. \]

3.2 Natural filtrations

The powers of the maximal ideal form a decreasing additive filtration

\[ \mathcal O_F\supset\mathfrak p_F\supset\mathfrak p_F^2\supset\cdots, \]

and the higher unit groups

\[ U_F^n=1+\mathfrak p_F^n, \qquad n\ge1, \]

form a decreasing multiplicative filtration

\[ \mathcal O_F^\times\supset U_F^1\supset U_F^2\supset\cdots. \]

3.3 Hensel’s lemma

Lemma 3.1 (Hensel’s lemma: factorization form) Let \(P\in\mathcal O_F[X]\) be monic. Suppose that its reduction modulo \(\mathfrak p_F\) factors as

\[ \overline P=\overline A\,\overline B \qquad\text{in }k_F[X], \]

where \(\overline A\) and \(\overline B\) are monic and relatively prime. Then there are unique monic polynomials \(A,B\in\mathcal O_F[X]\) satisfying

\[ A\bmod\mathfrak p_F=\overline A, \qquad B\bmod\mathfrak p_F=\overline B, \qquad P=AB. \]

Corollary 3.1 (Simple roots lift) Let \(P\in\mathcal O_F[X]\). If \(\overline a\in k_F\) is a simple root of \(\overline P\), then there is a unique \(a\in\mathcal O_F\) reducing to \(\overline a\) such that

\[ P(a)=0. \]

Apply Corollary 3.1 to \(P(X)=X^{q-1}-1\). Since \(p\nmid q-1\), every nonzero residue class is a simple root. Therefore reduction induces an isomorphism

\[ \mu_{q-1}(F)\xrightarrow{\sim}k_F^\times, \]

where

\[ \mu_{q-1}(F)=\{x\in F^\times:x^{q-1}=1\}. \]

The exact sequence

\[ 1\longrightarrow U_F^1 \longrightarrow\mathcal O_F^\times \longrightarrow k_F^\times \longrightarrow1 \]

therefore splits via the Teichmuller representatives \(\mu_{q-1}(F)\). Hence

\[ \mathcal O_F^\times \cong \mu_{q-1}(F)\times U_F^1 \cong k_F^\times\times U_F^1. \]

Example 3.1 (Prime-to-p roots of unity) For \(x\in F^\times\),

\[ x\in\mu_{q-1}(F) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\text{ has finite order relatively prime to }p. \]

We will use Hensel’s lemma. It suffices to show that the RHS implies the LHS.

Let \(x\in F^\times\) satisfy \(x^d=1\) for some \(d\) prime to \(p\). Then \(x\in\mathcal O_F^\times\), and \(\bar x^d=1\) in \(k_F^\times\). Since \(k_F^\times\) is cyclic of order \(q-1\), the order of \(\bar x\) divides \(d'=\gcd(d,q-1)\). Hence \(\bar x^{d'}=1\).

Set \(y=x^{d'}\). Then \(\bar y=1\) and \(y^{d/d'}=1\). Since \(d/d'\) is prime to \(p\), \(1\) is a simple root of \(X^{d/d'}-1\) modulo \(\mathfrak p_F\). By the uniqueness part of Hensel’s lemma, \(1\) is the unique root congruent to \(1\) modulo \(\mathfrak p_F\). Therefore \(y=1\), and hence \(x^{d'}=1\).

4 Linear changes of variables over a local field

Let \(F\) be any local field and give \(F^n\) the product additive Haar measure

\[ \mu_{F^n}=\mu_F^{\otimes n}. \]

Proposition 4.1 (Modulus of a linear automorphism) For every \(A\in\mathrm{GL}_n(F)\),

\[ \delta_{F^n}(A)=|\det A|_F, \]

where \(|\cdot|_F\) denotes the Haar-normalized absolute value of \(F\). Equivalently,

\[ \int_{F^n}f(Ax)\,d\mu_{F^n}(x) = |\det A|_F^{-1} \int_{F^n}f(x)\,d\mu_{F^n}(x). \]

For \(n=1\), this is exactly the definition of \(|\cdot|_F\). If

\[ A=P^{-1}DP \]

with \(D=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\), then multiplicativity of the automorphism modulus gives

\[ \delta_{F^n}(A)=\delta_{F^n}(D). \]

Using the product measure and changing one coordinate at a time,

\[ \delta_{F^n}(D) = \prod_{i=1}^n|a_i|_F = |\det D|_F = |\det A|_F. \]

Thus Proposition 4.1 holds whenever \(A\) is diagonalizable over \(F\).

Lemma 4.1 (Absolute values in finite extensions) If \(L/K\) is a finite extension of local fields, then for every \(x\in K\),

\[ |x|_L=|x|_K^{[L:K]}. \]

To see this, regard the multiplication map \(m_x:L\to L\) as the scalar linear map \(xI\) on the \([L:K]\)-dimensional \(K\)-vector space \(L\). The diagonal case already proved above shows that its modulus is \(|x|_K^{[L:K]}\).

Now suppose that \(A\) is semisimple and choose a finite splitting field \(L/F\). Put \(d=[L:F]\). After choosing an \(F\)-basis of \(L\), the action of \(A\) on the additive group \(L^n\) is the direct sum of \(d\) copies of its action on \(F^n\). Since any two Haar measures differ by a scalar, this identification gives

\[ \delta_{L^n}(A)=\delta_{F^n}(A)^d. \]

Over \(L\), the matrix \(A\) is diagonalizable. The diagonalizable case and Lemma 4.1 give

\[ \delta_{L^n}(A) =|\det A|_L =|\det A|_F^d. \]

Taking positive \(d\)th roots proves the desired identity over \(F\) for every semisimple \(A\). Finally, regular semisimple matrices are dense in \(\mathrm{GL}_n(F)\): their complement is the zero set of the nonzero discriminant polynomial and has empty interior over a local field. Both

\[ A\longmapsto\delta_{F^n}(A) \qquad\text{and}\qquad A\longmapsto|\det A|_F \]

are continuous. This proves Proposition 4.1 for every \(A\in\mathrm{GL}_n(F)\).

5 The upper-triangular group in \(\mathrm{GL}_2(F)\)

We now compute the formula announced in Example 1.4 over an arbitrary local field \(F\). Let

\[ B= \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a&b\\ 0&c \end{pmatrix} :a,c\in F^\times,\ b\in F \right\}. \]

Write

\[ T= \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} a&0\\ 0&c \end{pmatrix} :a,c\in F^\times \right\}, \qquad U= \left\{ \begin{pmatrix} 1&u\\ 0&1 \end{pmatrix} :u\in F \right\}. \]

Then \(B=U\rtimes T\). Both \(T\cong F^\times\times F^\times\) and \(U\cong(F,+)\) are abelian and hence unimodular. Conjugation is given by

\[ \begin{pmatrix} a&0\\ 0&c \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 1&u\\ 0&1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a&0\\ 0&c \end{pmatrix}^{-1} = \begin{pmatrix} 1&\dfrac ac\,u\\ 0&1 \end{pmatrix}. \]

Let \(d\mu_U(u)\) be additive Haar measure on \(F\), and let \(d\mu_T(t)\) be Haar measure on \(T\). A right Haar measure on \(B\) is characterized by

\[ \int_B f(b)\,d\mu_B(b) = \int_T\int_U f(ut)\,d\mu_U(u)\,d\mu_T(t), \qquad f\in C_c(B). \]

By the uniqueness of Haar measure, it suffices to show that \(d\mu_U\otimes d\mu_T\) is right invariant. Indeed, for \(u'\in U\) and \(t'\in T\), \[ (ut)(u't')=u(tu't^{-1})(tt'). \] Writing \(u''=tu't^{-1}\in U\), right translation by \(u't'\) sends \((u,t)\) to \((uu'',tt')\). Since \(d\mu_U\) is translation invariant and \(d\mu_T\) is right invariant, the product measure \(d\mu_U\otimes d\mu_T\) is right invariant.

Take

\[ g= \begin{pmatrix} x&y\\ 0&z \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1&y/z\\ 0&1 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x&0\\ 0&z \end{pmatrix}. \]

Under left multiplication by \(g\), the \(U\)-coordinate changes by

\[ u\longmapsto \frac yz+\frac xz\,u. \]

Translation does not change additive Haar measure, while multiplication by \(x/z\) scales it by \(|x/z|_F\). The \(T\)-coordinate is left-translated by \(\operatorname{diag}(x,z)\), which preserves \(d\mu_T\) because \(T\) is unimodular. Therefore

\[ \begin{aligned} \int_B f(gb)\,d\mu_B(b) &=\left|\frac{x}{z}\right|_F^{-1} \int_B f(b)\,d\mu_B(b). \end{aligned} \]

Comparing this with the defining formula for the modular character yields

\[ \boxed{ \delta_B\!\left( \begin{pmatrix} x&y\\ 0&z \end{pmatrix} \right) = \left|\frac{x}{z}\right|_F }. \]
